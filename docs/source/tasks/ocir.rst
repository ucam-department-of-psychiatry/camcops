Obsessive-Compulsive Inventory Revised (OCI-R)
----------------------------------------------

.. include:: include_data_collection_plus_local_upgrade.rst

The OCI-R is a 18-item self-report measure to assess symptoms of
obsessive–compulsive disorder (OCD).

Each item is a statement referring to an experience that many people have in
their everyday lives. The respondent indicates on a scale of 0 to 4 how much the
experience has distressed or bothered them in the last month, where 0 = "Not at
all" and 4 = "Extremely".

The 18 items compose six subscales:

- washing (items 5, 11, 17);
- checking (items 6, 12, 18);
- ordering (items 1, 7, 13);
- obsessing (items 3, 9, 15);
- hoarding (items 2, 8, 14);
- mental neutralising (items 4, 10, 16);

These are not stated explicitly in the source, but they can be inferred from Foa
et al. (2002).

The OCI-R is a revised version of the 42-item Obsessive-Compulsive Inventory
(Foa et al 1998), which measures both frequency and distressed caused by the
symptoms. The OCI-R eliminates the frequency scale, simplifies the scoring and
reduces overlap across sub-scales.

Source
~~~~~~

By email from CPFT. Identical to that hosted at the University of Washington
https://pcl.psychiatry.uw.edu/resources/ (Direct link
https://pcl.psychiatry.uw.edu/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/OCI-R-Scale-and-Manual.pdf)
where it was decribed as "commonly used and publicly available". MB contacted
Mollie Forrester (Director, Patient and Family Experience UoW) in November 2026
who confirmed with evidence that Dr Foa granted permission in April 2025 to post
the tool on their website and they have since updated the text on the website to
say "publicly available or used with permission of the developer or copyright
holder". They also confirmed that both the scale and manual had been sent to
them as separate documents from the CTSA.

The combined three-page document has the questions and the copyright notice on
page 1. The questions are identical to those in the Appendix of Foa et al (2002).

The OCI-R manual on pages 2 and 3 do not include a copyright notice and have
descriptions of the six sub-scales, calculation of the mean scores and this
text:

    Each score is presented as a mean out of a possible maximum of ‘4’. A total
    score of 18 or more, or a mean score of 2.5 or more in any of the subscales
    suggests the presence of OCD, but is not diagnostic.

They also have notes as to how the scale should be introduced to the patient and
descriptions of the severity scale (e.g. "2 = Moderately" is described as 2-3
times per week). These descriptions are not present in Foa et al. (2002).
